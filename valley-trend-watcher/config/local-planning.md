# Local Planning and House Building

The slowest-moving layer and the largest in pounds. Checked **monthly**, not
weekly, except when a major approval lands, which is a same-week flag.

## The standing big one: Selwood Garden Community

**1,700 homes on the south-western edge of Frome.** Approved by the Secretary of
State for Housing, Steve Reed, in **April 2026**, after the application was
called in (February 2025) and went to public inquiry. The government's decision
letter cited "a pressing and compelling need to boost the supply of market and
affordable housing" in the area.

What it includes: **510 affordable homes** (30% of supply), two care homes,
6.7 hectares of employment land, a mixed-use local centre, a primary school and
open spaces.

Who: **Grass Roots Planning Ltd**, on behalf of **Land Value Alliances** and a
landowners consortium. Architects **NVB**.

⚠️ **Start date is unconfirmed and sources conflict.** One local report says the
first homes could begin construction by spring 2026; an earlier one said work
could start around 18 months after approval. Do not state a start date.

**Why it matters to Valley, in order of how soon:**

1. **Site phase.** Hoarding and site fencing, temporary works, timber for
   welfare and storage. A trade relationship with whoever builds it is worth
   more than any single order.
2. **Landscaping and biodiversity net gain.** Planting, stakes, guards,
   hedgerow, retaining and sleepers. BNG conditions make this a requirement, not
   an optional extra.
3. **The long tail, and the big one.** 1,700 households over a decade, all doing
   fences, gardens, raised beds, decking, sheds and garden rooms. A new estate
   generates retail garden demand for twenty years. We are nine miles away.

Also live: **Saxonvale**, the Frome town centre regeneration.

## Self-build registers: the sharpest lead signal here

Both **Somerset Council** and **Frome Town Council** keep registers of people who
want to build their own home locally. These are people who, by definition, are
about to buy structural timber, cladding and joinery.

- [Somerset Council self-build and custom housebuilding guide](https://www.somerset.gov.uk/planning-buildings-and-land/sbchg120626p1/)
- [Frome Town Council self-build register](https://www.frometowncouncil.gov.uk/frome-self-build-register/)

The Right to Build (since April 2016) obliges councils to keep the register and
to have enough shovel-ready plots to meet the demand on it. The August 2026 NPPF
requires local plans to assess self-build need and identify plots. So the
register is growing, and councils have to act on it.

**What to watch:** register numbers if published, and any plot allocations coming
forward. **What to do with it:** this is a reason to have self-build-facing
content and a conversation with the council, not a list of names to approach.

## What to flag, and what to ignore

**Flag:**
- Any residential scheme of 10 homes or more inside the catchment
- Barn conversions and Class Q applications: rural, timber-heavy, often
  self-managed
- A rise in outbuilding and garden room applications, which is a volume signal
  for our best product story
- Landscaping and BNG conditions on large sites
- Council and parish tree planting, hedgerow and community growing schemes
- Changes to permitted development for outbuildings, the single biggest
  regulatory lever on us

**Ignore:** individual householder extensions, and anything where the only
"insight" is a named person's address.

> ⚠️ **The rule on using this.** Planning data is public, but it is about
> identifiable people and their homes. Use it to understand **where demand is
> forming and who to build a trade relationship with**. Never to target named
> individuals, and never put a named applicant or address in a post. If a
> finding only works by naming a private individual, drop it.
