## The three claims rules — get these wrong and we lose trust

**1. We do not make most of what we sell.**
Valley buys in the majority of its products from British and European suppliers
and mills a few specific niche products. The guide is explicit: do not pretend we
cut it all. What we are genuinely good at is knowing what to cut, what to source
and where to get the good stuff.

So: **never lead a post with "we mill it ourselves"** unless that product really
is Valley-milled. The valley-milled tag on the site marks roughly 37 products.
If in doubt, say we choose it and stock it, not that we made it.

**2. Never offer to make or cut something we have not confirmed we can.**
The guide says it plainly: do not suggest what else we could make or cut unless
you know for sure. This directly constrains the signal map. A **MACHINE** status
means "raise it internally as an opportunity", it does **not** mean "post that we
can do it". Mill opportunities are a note for Kev, never a public claim.

**3. The workshop and finishing services do not exist yet.**
Valley will have a workshop, full finishing services and workshop events. It
cannot offer them now. Never imply otherwise.

## Voice, in short

Write the way Garth, Caspar, Garry or Kev would say it at the counter. That rule
sits above everything.

- **Warm, plain, useful.** A knowledgeable neighbour leaning on the counter, not
  a brochure.
- **Say the useful thing first.** "Allow about 10% extra for waste", not "When
  considering the quantity required".
- **Demonstrate expertise, don't announce it.** Give an answer only someone who
  knows timber could.
- **Be comfortable saying "it depends."** Wood is not uniform.
- **Be happy to say what you would not use.** More credible than pretending
  everything is wonderful.
- **Do not romanticise wood.** No "beautiful natural material", no "timeless
  character". Talk about what it does.
- **Do not oversell.** No "unrivalled choice", no "simply the best".

## Mechanics

- **UK English.** -ise not -ize. UK dates: 24 August 2026.
- **No em-dashes or en-dashes standing in for them.** An aside becomes commas or
  brackets. An abrupt turn becomes a colon or a full stop. Do not swap in a
  spaced hyphen, which is a dash in disguise. En-dash only for real ranges: 9–5.
- **Numbers:** one to nine spelled out, 10 and up in numerals. Always numerals
  with units and prices: 3 planks, 20%, £8. Never start a sentence with a numeral.
- **No prices in product copy.** They go stale.
- **Sentence case headings.** No Title Case For Everything.
- **Contractions are fine and preferred:** here's, we'll, can't.
- **No emoji in body copy.** No exclamation marks unless it really is one.
- **No Oxford comma** by default, unless the sentence would be ambiguous.

## Specific word choices

- **"firewood", not "logs".** House rule, and SEO.
- **Fencing: consider "boundaries"** for SEO where it reads naturally.
- **Use:** use, cut, dry, move, last, fit, choose, keep, rot.
- **Avoid:** utilise, provide, ensure, suitable, requirements, solutions,
  selection.
- **Avoid the AI tells:** delve, leverage, seamless, robust, elevate, unlock,
  boasts, nestled, curated, bespoke (unless it literally is), "in today's world",
  "when it comes to", "at the end of the day", and "it's not just X, it's Y".
- **"our little mill"** is the softener when "sawmill" feels intimidating.

## Product pillars — use these names

**Build · Clad · Make · Grow · Landscape · Fence · Firewood**

Service pillars: mill · cut-to-size · species knowledge.

Brand line: *Where Materials Meet Their Makers.*
Show line: *Something's Happening at Valley.*

## The two copy tests

1. Would someone at Valley actually say this to a customer at the counter? If
   not, simplify it.
2. Does this sentence help the customer choose, buy, build or look after
   something? If not, we probably don't need it.
