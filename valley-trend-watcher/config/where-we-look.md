# Where We Look for Trends

**Outward for the signal, inward for whether we can serve it.** These are two
different questions and the watcher must not confuse them.

The catchment rings (20 miles for firewood and boundaries, 60 for cladding,
slabs and structural) decide **who we sell to**. They say nothing about where a
trend comes from. A look that starts in Copenhagen or on a Japanese timber
account reaches a Somerset garden soon enough, and the advantage is in seeing it
early.

So: source globally, judge locally.

## Layer 1 — Global design and build

Where the look is invented, usually two to five years ahead of the UK high
street.

- **Architecture and design press:** Dezeen, ArchDaily, Architects' Journal.
  Watch for what materials keep appearing, not individual buildings.
- **Nordic and Japanese timber culture.** Shou sugi ban (charred timber),
  Japandi interiors, Nordic sauna culture, outdoor bathing. Sauna in particular
  has gone from niche to mainstream in the UK in about three years and is a
  cladding and structural story.
- **Mass timber and CLT**, passive house, low-carbon construction. Slow-moving,
  but it changes what architects specify, which changes what trade asks for.
- **Biophilic design, outdoor rooms, outdoor kitchens.** American and Australian
  led, lands here with a lag.
- **Pinterest Predicts** and similar annual forecasts. Free, and genuinely early
  on domestic looks.

## Layer 2 — UK national

- **Television.** See `shows.md`. Mainstream first.
- **Press and magazines:** Grand Designs Magazine, Gardeners' World magazine,
  Houzz UK's annual reports, the weekend property and garden supplements.
- **Trade press:** Timber Trades Journal, Builders Merchants News, Horticulture
  Week. What the trade is worried about becomes what customers ask about.
- **Google Trends UK.** The cheapest proof we have that a look is converting
  into search.
- **RHS and the show season.** Chelsea sets the year's garden language.

## Layer 3 — Platform and social

YouTube (explicit intent, measurable), Instagram, Pinterest Trends, TikTok. See
`social-sources.md` for what each can actually give us.

## Layer 4 — Comparator towns, the Frome-like places

**This is the sharpest local-but-not-hyperlocal signal we have.**

Frome has a particular character: independent-minded, maker and repair culture,
strong independent high street, sustainability-conscious, a mix of older
downsizers with money and younger families doing things themselves. A handful of
UK towns share that character almost exactly, and a trend that lands in one
usually lands in the others.

These are **not** customers. They're a reading of what our own customers will
want next.

**The core comparators:** Totnes, Stroud, Hebden Bridge, Lewes, Glastonbury,
Hay-on-Wye, Todmorden, Machynlleth, Ashburton, Crickhowell, Forest Row, Bungay.
Plus the independent pockets of Bristol (Bedminster, Totterdown) and Bath.

**What to watch in them:**

- What's opening and closing on the high street, especially makers, workshops,
  repair cafés, zero-waste and garden shops.
- Community build projects: community orchards, growing schemes, men's sheds,
  school and allotment builds, skate parks, community saunas. These are visible,
  photographable and often timber-heavy.
- Local makers and small builders, and what they're posting.
- Local press and the town's own Facebook groups and noticeboards.
- Council and parish schemes: tree planting, hedgerow, biodiversity work.

**Why it works for us:** Totnes built a community sauna before most of the
country had heard of one. Stroud's farmers' market culture ran ahead of the
national trend by years. These towns are an early-warning system that costs
nothing to read.

Frome is itself one of these towns, so this is also about not being last in our
own category.

## Layer 5 — Regulation and structural change

Slow, unglamorous, and the biggest mover of all. Worth a check monthly rather
than weekly.

- **Permitted development for outbuildings and garden rooms.** Any loosening is
  a direct hit on our best story.
- **DEFRA and Ready to Burn rules**, wood burner regulation, clean air zones.
  Directly shapes the firewood trade.
- **Future Homes Standard and building regs.** Changes what gets specified.
- **Biodiversity net gain.** Pushes landscaping, planting and the timber around
  it.
- **Energy costs.** The single biggest driver of firewood demand, and it moves
  with the price cap announcements.

## Layer 6 — Weather and climate

Met Office warnings for the fast signal, seasonal outlooks for the slow one. A
cold winter forecast is a firewood stocking decision. See `social-sources.md`.

## How to weigh it

1. **Global and national layers tell us what's coming.** Use them for the weeks
   ahead section and for stock and range decisions.
2. **Comparator towns tell us when it's arriving.** A thing showing up in Totnes
   and Stroud is close.
3. **Local and weather tell us what to do this week.**

A trend we spot globally but can't serve is still worth recording. A trend
already visible in Frome is late.
