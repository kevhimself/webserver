# Valley - Values-Aligned Product Data & Pricing Strategy

## Our Values and How They Drive Every Decision

| Value | How it shows up in product data & pricing |
|-------|------------------------------------------|
| **Quality** | We only list products we'd use ourselves. Data includes why our version is better, not just what it is. |
| **Integrity** | Every price is traceable back to cost. No hidden markups. Our margins are fair and we can explain them. |
| **Fairness** | Fair for customers (competitive, transparent) AND fair for us (reflects expertise, service, full-job support). |
| **Openness** | Customers see what they're getting. Product info includes fitting advice, care instructions, what else they need. |
| **Consistency** | Same product = same name, same format, same pricing logic everywhere (POS, website, price sheet, showroom). |
| **Trust** | Data is right. If the label says £5.99, the POS says £5.99, the website says £5.99. No surprises. |
| **Helpful** | Product data tells you what you need to know to make a good decision, not just what we want to sell. |
| **Getting the whole job done** | Every product links to related items: what else you need to complete the project properly. |
| **Maintain, nurture and care** | Product info includes ongoing care guidance. We sell the long game, not the quick transaction. |
| **Make good decisions now, do less later** | Invest in clean data once. Automate updates. Don't rebuild from scratch every season. |
| **Use resources better** | Share data across all channels from one source. No duplicate spreadsheets, no conflicting prices. |

## Pricing Strategy: The Valley Way

### Core Pricing Formula

```
COST PRICE (what we pay)
  + MARGIN (what we need to earn - typically 30-50% depending on category)
  = BASE SELL PRICE
  → Apply rounding rules (customer-friendly numbers)
  → Compare to competitors
  → Adjust if needed (with documented reason)
  = FINAL SELL PRICE
```

### Margin Guidelines by Category

| Category | Target Margin | Rationale |
|----------|--------------|-----------|
| Timber (commodity) | 30-35% | Price-sensitive, high volume, customers compare |
| Fencing (systems) | 35-45% | Full-job value, advice, selection help |
| Decking | 35-45% | Design input, quality differentiation |
| Sleepers | 30-40% | Commodity end but quality matters |
| Fixings & accessories | 45-55% | Convenience, small ticket, bundled value |
| Add-ons (caps, oils, tools) | 45-60% | Often overlooked by customer in initial price comparison |
| Services | 40-50% | Pure expertise value |

### Pricing Rules (Values-Aligned)

1. **The Fairness Check**
   - Would I feel OK explaining this price to a customer face-to-face? If not, adjust.
   - Does the margin reflect the genuine value we add (advice, selection, full-job support)?

2. **The Rounding Rule**
   - Round to customer-friendly numbers: £X.99, £X.49, or whole pounds for bigger items
   - Never round UP more than 5% from calculated price without documenting why

3. **The Competitor Rule**
   - Track 5 key competitors: Haven Timber, Charltons Timber, Fountain Forestry, B&Q, Amazon
   - For reference products (sleepers, 5'6" featheredge, postcrete): stay within 5-10% of market
   - For everything else: price on VALUE, not just cost. We're not competing to be cheapest.

4. **The Differentiator Rule**
   - When customer thinks they're comparing like-for-like, but they're not (treatment quality, grading, origin, service), DOCUMENT the difference
   - This feeds into: sales team talking points, showroom info, website product descriptions

5. **The Loss Leader Rule**
   - Identify 5-10 reference products customers always price-check (postcrete, sleepers, basic fence panels)
   - Price these competitively (even thin margin) to earn the visit
   - Recover margin on the full-job add-ons (caps, fixings, treatment, delivery)

6. **The Pret Rule (Generosity Strategy)**
   - Lead with free value first: free delivery over threshold, free advice, free cutting
   - Discount as last resort, not first offer
   - "We'll throw in delivery" feels more generous than "10% off" (and costs us less)

### Smart Pricing Tactics

| Tactic | Example | When to use |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| Bundle pricing | "Complete fence kit: panels + posts + fixings" | High-value projects, full-job sales |
| Volume breaks | "10+ sleepers: £X each" | Bulk/trade buyers |
| Per-metre vs per-item | Show both where relevant | Helps comparison shoppers |
| Project pricing | "6ft fence, 10m run = £XXX" | Website calculators, quotes |
| Seasonal | "Spring fencing offer" | Stock management, seasonal demand |

## Competitor Comparison Approach

### Reference Products (Track Weekly)

These are the products customers will price-check before visiting. Keep these sharp.

| Product | Why it's a reference | Comparison note |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| 6' x 5'6" featheredge panel | Everyone's first fence question | Quality/thickness varies hugely |
| Sleepers (new, treated) | Landscape staple | Size/treatment/grade varies |
| Postcrete 20kg | Every fence job needs it | True commodity, price matters |
| 3" x 3" fence post 8ft | Goes with every panel | Treatment and quality vary |
| Decking board (standard) | Entry point to decking | Huge quality range |

### How to Compare

1. **Price match the product** - What do they charge for the equivalent?
2. **Match the specification** - Is it actually equivalent? (thickness, treatment, grade, origin)
3. **Match the service** - Do they deliver? Cut to size? Advise? Stock it reliably?
4. **Document the gaps** - "B&Q sleeper is X treatment, ours is Y. Theirs will last 5 years, ours 15."

### Differentiator Cheat Sheet (for sales team)

| What customer says | What they're probably comparing | Our answer |
|---|---|---|
| "B&Q is cheaper" | Thinner boards, lower grade timber, no advice | "Their featheredge is Xmm, ours is Xmm. Ours will last X years longer." |
| "I found it online for less" | Delivery cost not included, can't see quality | "Come see and feel the difference. We'll match on genuine like-for-like." |
| "Your sleeper costs more" | Different treatment/grade/size | "Ours are [grade], [treatment]. Here's what that means for your garden in 5 years." |

## Data Quality Rules (Non-Negotiable)

These protect the **trust** value. If the data isn't right, nothing else matters.

1. **Every product has:** name, SKU, category, unit, cost price, sell price, supplier, last updated date
2. **Every price has:** a traceable cost source (invoice reference or supplier price list)
3. **No price goes live** without a second pair of eyes (human review)
4. **Price changes are logged** - what changed, when, why, who approved
5. **Weekly spot-check** - pick 10 random products, verify POS price = source price = website price
6. **If in doubt, check** - better to delay than to publish a wrong price

## How AI Helps (and Where It Doesn't)

### AI Does Well
- Extracting data from supplier invoices/price lists (OCR + structuring)
- Calculating margins, comparing prices, formatting data
- Scraping competitor prices (with supervision)
- Generating product descriptions from specifications
- Formatting data for Lightspeed/WooCommerce import
- Flagging anomalies ("this margin is 80%, is that right?")
- Creating care and fitting guides from product knowledge

### AI Doesn't Replace
- Final pricing decisions (human judgement on what's fair)
- Product selection (what should we stock and why)
- Customer relationships (the conversation in the showroom)
- Quality assessment (touching the timber, seeing the grade)
- Local market knowledge (what Godalming/Surrey customers actually want)
- The story (why Valley exists, what makes it different)

### The Human-AI Partnership Model

```
SUPPLIER INVOICE ARRIVES
  → AI extracts prices, quantities, product details
  → AI calculates new margins, flags changes from last batch
  → AI compares to competitors
  → AI generates recommendation: "suggest £X.XX (margin Y%, vs competitor Z at £A.AA)"

  → HUMAN reviews, adjusts, approves
  → HUMAN adds context ("good batch, premium grade, price accordingly")
  → HUMAN makes final call

  → SYSTEM updates POS, website, price sheets automatically
  → SYSTEM logs the change with human's approval note
```
