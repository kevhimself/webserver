How the tariff works

We're on Octopus Flux. The price changes three times a day, for buying and for selling.

2am to 5amCheap buy 16.1p, sell 4.7p
4pm to 7pmPeak buy 37.2p, sell 30.2p
The restStandard buy 26.7p, sell 11.1p

Southern England rates, October 2026, including VAT.

Why this suits a battery. The battery can only charge or discharge at 3 kW, so in the three cheap hours it takes in about 9 kWh, which is almost exactly a full battery. That stored power then runs the house through the day and, most importantly, from 4pm to 7pm, when buying is at its dearest. On a sunny day there's less to buy, so the planner leaves room for the solar instead.
Who decides. A free planner called Predbat runs in Home Assistant. Every few minutes it looks at prices, the solar forecast and how much we usually use, then decides how full to charge overnight. When Octopus offers a free hour, we tell Predbat that hour costs 0p and it fills the battery then.

The system

Fitted in April 2023. The panels and battery are two separate systems that meet on the house wiring.

HouseBuilt 1976, Godalming, Surrey. Gas boiler for heating and hot water.
Solar panels9 × 420 W (3.78 kWp): 6 facing east, 3 facing south. Some shade from a tree line.
Panel optimisersSolarEdge, one per panel, so a shaded panel doesn't drag the rest down.
Solar inverterSolarEdge HD-Wave, 3.68 kW.
BatteryGivEnergy 9.5 kWh with a 3 kW AC charger.
Hot waterCylinder heated by the gas boiler, a SolarEdge solar diverter (summer only) and a Heata unit, a computer server whose waste heat warms the water.
TariffOctopus Flux for buying and selling.
BrainHome Assistant on a Home Assistant Green, with Predbat planning the battery.
Solar per yearAbout 3,000 kWh, nearly all of it March to September.
What "AC-coupled" means here. The solar inverter turns the panels' power into normal mains electricity and feeds the house. The battery has its own charger, which watches the meter: when the house is sending power out to the grid, it soaks that up instead. So the battery never "sees" the panels directly. That made it easy to add a battery without replacing the solar kit, at the cost of two apps and a 3 kW limit.
Why the diverter only runs on a timer. A solar diverter is meant to send spare solar into the hot water tank. Ours has no meter to see when there's spare, so it just runs a short boost at midday in summer and is switched off in winter, when the boiler does the work.

What we've learned

The useful bits from setting this up in autumn 2026, in case they save someone else the trouble.

  1. The battery was charging every night for nothing
    On a flat-rate tariff, the battery maker's cloud was filling it from the grid each night. Same price day and night, so that only lost 10–15% in charging losses and wore the battery. Lesson: find out what's controlling your battery, and allow only one controller.
  2. One wrong setting cost 20 hours of grid charging
    The battery's "reserve" got set to 67% by mistake, so it topped itself up from the grid to stay there. We added an alert for when the reserve or grid charging goes outside what we expect.
  3. Read the panels locally, not through the cloud
    The solar installer couldn't give us access to the online data, so Home Assistant reads the inverter directly over the home network (Modbus). It's faster and doesn't depend on anyone's servers.
  4. A time-of-use tariff is what makes a battery pay
    On a flat price, a battery only stores solar. Once prices vary through the day, it can buy cheap at night and avoid the expensive evening hours. We moved to Octopus Flux in October 2026 and switched Predbat from watching to controlling.
  5. Two controllers can share, carefully
    We earn from grid "events" through Axle Energy, which can take over the battery for an hour when the grid is stretched. We set Axle to events only, so Predbat runs the battery the rest of the time, and we log every event to spot any clashes.
  6. Free and cheap hours are worth planning for
    Octopus sometimes offers free electricity for an hour. We tell Predbat that hour is free so it fills the battery, and run the dishwasher, washing and cooking in it.
  7. Understanding beats labels
    Knowing how something works ("Eco washes run cooler for longer, because heating water is what costs") makes the right choice obvious. We try to explain everything on this page that way.

How this page works

No server at home is opened to the internet. Home Assistant sends a small summary out; nothing comes back in.

Solar inverter and battery→ Home Assistant (at home)→ every 3 hours GitHub (stores the history)→ GitHub Pages (serves this page)

The page is plain HTML and JavaScript with Chart.js. The data is one JSON file, data/home.json, which anyone can read.

What we don't share. No street address, no account, meter or serial numbers, no live location, and nothing that would let anyone control the house. Just energy totals and a few readings.
Make your own. This page was inspired by our friend Tim's Open Norney, a forkable record of his off-grid bungalow. The same idea works for any home with Home Assistant: a summary sent out on a timer, a JSON file and one HTML page.
